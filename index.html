Process Optimization at King Abdulaziz International Airport

This capstone project applied Lean Management and the DMAIC framework to the passenger check-in process at KAIA Terminal 1, operated by Saudi Ground Services. The goal: cut processing time, reduce queues, and balance counter workload — all validated with real field data and passenger surveys.

Approach

Using the DMAIC cycle, I mapped the current-state process with Value Stream Mapping, ran time-and-motion studies and staff interviews to define the true sources of waste, then piloted interventions — self-service kiosks, digital ID verification, 5S counter organization, and dynamic counter allocation — before measuring the results against the baseline.

Key Results

  • Average total check-in time dropped from 15.5 to 9.2 minutes.
  • Queue waiting time was roughly halved through kiosks and dynamic counter allocation.
  • Document verification time fell by 57% after moving to digital ID/passport checks.
  • Off-peak counter utilization improved from around 50% to 70%, easing peak-hour strain.
  • Both passenger satisfaction and staff engagement scores improved post-intervention.

Recommendations

The study recommends scaling self-service kiosks and mobile check-in, institutionalizing biometric document verification, running weekly 5S audits, deploying real-time KPI dashboards for supervisors, and extending Lean methods to baggage handling and boarding — turning this pilot into a repeatable framework across the airport.
